# From a search record to a Review argument

Adapted from the search, screening, extraction and synthesis workflow in K-Dense
Scientific Agent Skills `literature-review` at the revision recorded in
`UPSTREAM.md`. The original MIT source is archived under `sources/kdense`.

Write down the Review question, audience, coverage dates and explicit boundaries
before searching. Match effort to the article type: a narrative Review is not a
systematic review merely because it searched several databases.

Use `nature-academic-search` and `citation-management` with more than one relevant
database when coverage matters. Record exact queries, database/provider, dates,
filters and result counts. Keep publisher and preprint versions linked; deduplicate
by identifiers and then title/authorship, retaining uncertain matches for review.
Use public topic terms for discovery. Do not transmit unpublished manuscript text
or reviewer comments outside the deployment's authorized processing boundary.

For systematic or scoping reviews, settle eligibility and exclusion criteria before
screening; preserve exclusion reasons and counts. Record protocol deviations. Use
the current appropriate reporting checklist and the review companion's
`ocsl-review-systematic-review` for an audit. Do not invent missing screening counts
or imply independent dual screening when it did not happen.

Build an extraction table with source/version/locator, research question, design,
population or benchmark, analysis unit, intervention or method, comparator,
outcomes, uncertainty, limitations and the exact claim supported. Mark unavailable
full text and use an unresolved state where abstracts cannot answer the question.
Do not infer supporting evidence from a title or metadata.

Synthesize by the governing question and meaningful themes, rather than one
paragraph per paper. Explain agreements, conflicting findings and conditions that
may explain differences. Track shared datasets, cohorts, authors and derivative
studies so repeated analyses are not mistaken for independent replication.
Distinguish demonstrated capabilities from proposals, preliminary results and
absence within the sources actually checked.

Keep the review-article brief authoritative for structure while allowing the
evidence to change its conclusions. If findings invalidate the proposed thesis,
record and resolve that conflict instead of selectively retaining favorable sources.
Use displays only when they help compare evidence or explain the argument; there
is no required number of AI-generated figures or required paid service.

The output should include a source/search log, an evidence extraction table,
a claim-linked thematic synthesis and unresolved gaps. Separate source identity,
automated consistency checks and human scientific verification in the report.
