# Gate 3 — Auto-Validation Checklist

English translation by OC Science Lab, 2026-09-09; original terms apply.

Run this checklist after completing the research text. Each item must pass; identify missing items explicitly.

## Required checks

### Citation completeness
- [ ] Every factual claim has a citation.
- [ ] Every citation exists in the reference list.
- [ ] Every reference-list entry is cited in the text.
- [ ] Key numbers and parameters have sources, including corrosion rates, temperatures and compositions.

### Method reproducibility
- [ ] Materials and reagents include brand, purity and batch where applicable.
- [ ] Equipment models and key parameters are specified.
- [ ] Conditions include temperature, time, atmosphere and pressure.
- [ ] Replicate counts are explicit.
- [ ] Statistical methods are identified.
- [ ] Outlier-exclusion criteria are defined.

### Terminology
- [ ] Abbreviations are expanded on first use.
- [ ] Domain-specific terms are defined or sourced on first use.
- [ ] Variables and symbols are explained.

### Logical chain
- [ ] The Discussion addresses the questions posed in the Introduction.
- [ ] Each objective or hypothesis has a corresponding experiment or analysis.
- [ ] Conclusions stay within the data's support.
- [ ] Novelty claims such as “this study is the first” have literature support.

### Figures and tables
- [ ] Numbering is consecutive.
- [ ] Cross-references are correct.
- [ ] Each figure and table has a title.
- [ ] Axes have labels and units.
- [ ] Legends are clear.

### Formatting
- [ ] Reference formatting is consistent.
- [ ] Numbers and units use consistent formatting, such as °C rather than mixed °C, ℃ and deg C.
- [ ] Chemical-formula subscripts and superscripts are correct: MgCl₂, not MgCl2.

## Assessment

- Pass = ✅.
- Missing but quickly repairable = ⚠️, identifying the missing item.
- Structural omission = ❌: fail and return to the relevant stage.

## Language-specific checks

For English manuscripts also check:
- [ ] Spelling and grammar.
- [ ] Tense consistency: past tense in Methods and Results; Discussion may mix tenses.

For Chinese manuscripts also check:
- [ ] Redundant repeated possessive/attributive particles, particularly consecutive uses of *de*.
- [ ] Europeanized syntax, particularly excessively long attributive clauses.
